Q: Tell me about Mercury.
A: Great question! "Mercury" is one of the most versatile names in science and mythology. Here are the three most prominent meanings: --- MERCURY THE PLANET Mercury is the first planet from the Sun and the smallest in the Solar System. It is a rocky planet with a surface that closely resembles Earth's Moon, covered in craters, rupes (cliff-like thrust faults), and bright ray systems formed by ejected material from impacts. Its largest crater, Caloris Planitia, stretches 1,550 km (960 mi) across, about one-third of the planet's own diameter of 4,880 km (3,030 mi). A Mercurian year lasts only 88 Earth days, but due to its unusual 3:2 spin-orbit resonance, a single solar day (sunrise to sunrise) lasts about 176 Earth days. This means one side of Mercury bakes in sunlight for 88 Earth days straight, then sits in total darkness for the next 88. Surface temperatures swing wildly as a result, from -170 °C at night to 420 °C in direct sunlight. Mercury has a very thin exosphere and a weak magnetic field. Its poles are permanently shadowed, strongly suggesting the presence of water ice. Mercury formed about 4.5 billion years ago and is expected to eventually be destroyed when the Sun becomes a red giant in roughly 7 to 8 billion years. The first successful flyby of Mercury was by Mariner 10 in 1974, and it has since been explored by the MESSENGER and BepiColombo orbiters. --- MERCURY THE ELEMENT Mercury (chemical symbol Hg, atomic number 80) is a chemical element commonly known as quicksilver. It is the only metallic element that is liquid at standard temperature and pressure, and it has both the lowest freezing point and the lowest boiling point of any metal. It occurs naturally mostly as cinnabar (mercuric sulfide), and the pigment vermilion is derived from it. Mercury has been used in thermometers, barometers, fluorescent lamps, switches, and dental amalgam, though its use has declined significantly due to its toxicity. Exposure to mercury is toxic to the nervous system, immune system, and kidneys of humans and animals. --- MERCURY THE ROMAN GOD Mercury (Latin: Mercurius) is a major god in Roman religion and one of the 12 Dii Consentes, the principal deities of the Roman pantheon. He is the god of boundaries, commerce, communication, eloquence, financial gain, languages, luck, thieves, travelers, and trickery, and he also serves as the guide of souls to the underworld. He was the son of Maia and Jupiter, and is closely associated with the Greek god Hermes, sharing many of the same characteristics. Here is why: The argument has the following structure: Premise 1: All roses are flowers. Premise 2: Some flowers fade quickly. Conclusion: Therefore, all roses fade quickly. This is a logical fallacy known as the "undistributed middle" or an invalid syllogism. The word "some" in Premise 2 is the key problem. "Some flowers fade quickly" only tells us that at least one flower fades quickly, but it says nothing specific about roses. The flowers that fade quickly could be an entirely different subset of flowers that does not overlap with roses at all. To illustrate: imagine flowers are divided into roses and daisies. If only daisies fade quickly, then Premise 1 (all roses are flowers) and Premise 2 (some flowers, i.e., daisies, fade quickly) are both true, yet the conclusion (all roses fade quickly) is false. For the conclusion to be valid, Premise 2 would need to say something stronger, such as "All flowers fade quickly" or specifically "All roses fade quickly." With "some," the conclusion simply does not follow.
